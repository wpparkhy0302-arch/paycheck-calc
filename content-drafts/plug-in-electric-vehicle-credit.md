## Beyond the New-EV Headlines: Plug-in Hybrids and Used EVs

Most coverage of the EV tax credit focuses on brand-new battery electric vehicles, but the credit rules also specifically cover **plug-in hybrids (PHEVs)** and, separately, a whole different credit for **used EVs** — each with its own quirks worth understanding.

## Plug-in Hybrids: The Battery Size Threshold

A plug-in hybrid qualifies for the same **New Clean Vehicle Credit** as a full EV, up to $7,500, but the exact amount depends on meeting critical mineral and battery component sourcing requirements — a PHEV meeting only one of the two requirements typically caps out at **$3,750** rather than the full $7,500. Unlike a full EV, a PHEV must also have a battery capacity of **at least 7 kWh** to qualify at all; many smaller PHEV battery packs fall under this line and get zero credit.

## Price Caps Still Apply to PHEVs

| Vehicle Type | MSRP Cap |
|---|---|
| Van, SUV, pickup truck | $80,000 |
| Other vehicles (sedans, etc.) | $55,000 |

A plug-in hybrid SUV priced at $82,000 is disqualified entirely by the price cap, regardless of its battery specs.

## Income Limits (Same for New EVs and PHEVs)

| Filing Status | MAGI Limit |
|---|---|
| Single | $150,000 |
| Head of Household | $225,000 |
| Married Filing Jointly | $300,000 |

You can use **either the current year's or the prior year's MAGI**, whichever is lower — a useful planning detail if your income varies year to year.

## The Used EV / Used PHEV Credit Is a Separate, Smaller Benefit

The **Used Clean Vehicle Credit** covers vehicles (including qualifying used PHEVs) that are at least 2 model years old, purchased from a **licensed dealer** (not a private sale), for **$25,000 or less**. The credit is **30% of the sale price, capped at $4,000**.

### Worked Example

A buyer purchases a 3-year-old plug-in hybrid from a dealer for $19,000. 30% of $19,000 = $5,700, but the credit is capped at $4,000, so the buyer claims **$4,000**.

## Used EV Income Limits Are Lower

| Filing Status | MAGI Limit (Used Vehicle Credit) |
|---|---|
| Single | $75,000 |
| Head of Household | $112,500 |
| Married Filing Jointly | $150,000 |

Note these are noticeably lower than the new-vehicle thresholds — a common point of confusion for buyers assuming the same limits apply.

## Point-of-Sale Transfer

Since 2024, both credits can be **transferred to the dealer at the time of purchase**, directly reducing the price you pay instead of waiting to claim it on your tax return — but the dealer must be registered with the IRS to offer this, and you still need to meet the income requirements (if you don't, you may have to repay the transferred amount).
