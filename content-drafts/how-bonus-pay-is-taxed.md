## The Bonus Isn't Taxed More — It's Withheld Differently

Few paycheck events generate more confusion than a bonus that seems to vanish into withholding. The IRS treats bonuses as **"supplemental wages,"** and supplemental wages have their own special withholding rules, separate from your regular paycheck.

## Two IRS-Approved Withholding Methods

**The Percentage Method (flat rate):** If your bonus is paid separately from your regular wages, your employer can simply withhold a flat **22%** federal rate (37% on amounts over $1 million in a calendar year), regardless of your actual tax bracket.

**The Aggregate Method:** If your bonus is paid *together* with your regular paycheck, your employer may instead add it to your regular wages for that period, calculate withholding on the combined total using standard W-4 tables, then subtract what would've been withheld on regular wages alone. This often results in higher *apparent* withholding on the bonus check than the flat 22%.

## Worked Example: Flat Rate Method

A $5,000 bonus paid as a separate check:

- Federal withholding (22% flat): $1,100.00
- Social Security (6.2%, assuming under the wage base): $310.00
- Medicare (1.45%): $72.50
- State tax (varies — e.g., 5% flat state): $250.00
- **Net bonus received: ≈ $3,267.50**

## Why 22% Withholding Isn't Your Final Tax Rate

If your actual marginal tax bracket is lower than 22% (e.g., you're in the 12% bracket), too much was withheld on the bonus, and you'll get that difference back as part of your refund when you file. If your bracket is higher (e.g., 32% or 35%), you may owe additional tax at filing time — the flat 22% withholding is just an IRS-mandated estimate, not your real liability.

## FICA Still Applies in Full

Unlike federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare are withheld on bonuses at the same standard rates as regular wages — there's no special reduced or flat FICA rate for supplemental wages.

## Strategies If You Know a Bonus Is Coming

- Adjust your W-4 to withhold slightly more or less from regular paychecks to balance out a predictable annual bonus
- Increase 401(k) contributions specifically from the bonus check (if your plan allows) to reduce the taxable amount and boost retirement savings simultaneously
- Don't assume the withheld amount is wrong just because it looks like "half the bonus is gone" — compare it to your actual marginal rate at tax time
