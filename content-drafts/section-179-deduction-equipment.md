## Deduct the Whole Cost Now, Instead of a Little Each Year

Normally, buying business equipment means **depreciating** it — spreading the deduction over several years as the asset's useful life. **Section 179** lets qualifying self-employed workers and small businesses deduct the **entire purchase price in the year the equipment is placed in service** instead.

## The 2025 Limits

| Limit | Amount |
|---|---|
| Maximum Section 179 deduction | $1,250,000 |
| Phaseout begins at total equipment purchases of | $3,130,000 |
| Deduction limited to | Your business's net taxable income (can't create a loss) |

For the vast majority of freelancers and small business owners, the phaseout threshold is irrelevant — it's designed to limit the benefit for very large businesses, not typical sole proprietors.

## What Qualifies

- Computers, laptops, and office equipment
- Furniture (desks, chairs, filing cabinets)
- Machinery and business equipment (cameras, tools, specialized gear)
- Certain vehicles used over 50% for business (with special, lower limits for passenger vehicles)
- Off-the-shelf software

## What Doesn't Qualify

- Land and buildings (real property generally isn't eligible, with narrow exceptions for certain improvements)
- Equipment used 50% or less for business
- Property acquired from a related party (e.g., buying your own old equipment back from a family member's business)

## Worked Example

A freelance videographer buys a new camera rig, lenses, and editing computer for **$9,200** in 2025, used 100% for business.

- Without Section 179: deducted gradually over 5 years (~$1,840/year under standard depreciation)
- With Section 179: **entire $9,200 deducted in 2025**

At a combined ~35% marginal rate (federal + SE tax), that's roughly **$3,220 in tax savings in year one**, instead of spread thin over five years.

## Why Timing the Deduction Matters

Section 179 is most valuable in a year when you have **high taxable income** you want to offset immediately. If your business had a slow year with low profit, the deduction may be limited (it can't exceed your net business income) — in that case, standard depreciation spread across future years might actually serve you better.

## Section 179 vs. Bonus Depreciation

Bonus depreciation is a related but separate provision that can also allow significant first-year write-offs and, unlike Section 179, isn't limited by business income — the two are often used together, with Section 179 applied first.

## The Business-Use Requirement Doesn't End at Purchase

If you deduct equipment under Section 179 and later drop below 50% business use before its depreciation period ends, you may have to "recapture" (add back) part of the deduction as income.
