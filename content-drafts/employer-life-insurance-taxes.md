## The $50,000 Rule Nobody Reads Their Pay Stub Closely Enough to Notice

Many employees assume employer-provided group life insurance is simply a free perk with no tax consequence. For coverage up to $50,000, that's true. Above that, the IRS treats part of it as taxable income — and most employees never notice the line item explaining why.

## How It Works

Under **IRC Section 79**, an employer can provide up to **$50,000** of group term life insurance coverage completely tax-free to the employee. Any coverage *above* $50,000 creates **imputed income**: the IRS calculates a theoretical value for that excess coverage using its own age-based table, and that value is added to your taxable wages — even though you never receive the money in cash.

## The IRS Table (Cost Per $1,000 of Coverage, Per Month)

| Age | Monthly Cost per $1,000 |
|---|---|
| Under 25 | $0.05 |
| 25–29 | $0.06 |
| 30–34 | $0.08 |
| 35–39 | $0.09 |
| 40–44 | $0.10 |
| 45–49 | $0.15 |
| 50–54 | $0.23 |
| 55–59 | $0.43 |
| 60–64 | $0.66 |

## Worked Example

A 52-year-old employee with $150,000 in employer-provided group life insurance:

- Excess coverage: $150,000 − $50,000 = $100,000
- Monthly imputed cost: 100 × $0.23 = $23.00
- Annual imputed income added to W-2 Box 1: $23.00 × 12 = **$276.00**

This $276 is added to taxable wages for the year and subject to federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare — but it does **not** appear in your net pay, because you never actually received cash. You'll notice it as a slight gap between gross pay and what seems like your "real" salary on your W-2.

## Why Employers Still Offer It

Even with the imputed income tax, group life insurance through an employer is usually far cheaper per dollar of coverage than an individual policy, especially since most employers don't require a medical exam for amounts up to a guaranteed issue limit.

## Supplemental Life Insurance You Pay For

If you buy *additional* voluntary life insurance through payroll beyond what your employer provides free, those premiums are typically paid post-tax — meaning the eventual death benefit is received tax-free by your beneficiaries, consistent with how life insurance proceeds are generally treated.
