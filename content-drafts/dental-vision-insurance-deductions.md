## Smaller Premiums, Same Tax Rules

Dental and vision coverage is usually the cheapest line item on your pay stub, but it follows the exact same pre-tax mechanics as medical insurance when offered under your employer's **Section 125 cafeteria plan**.

## Typical Costs

| Coverage | Typical Employee Monthly Cost |
|---|---|
| Dental (individual) | $10–$25 |
| Dental (family) | $30–$60 |
| Vision (individual) | $4–$10 |
| Vision (family) | $12–$25 |

Small as they look, these deductions reduce your taxable wages for federal income tax, most state income tax, *and* FICA — exactly like a medical premium.

## Worked Example

An employee paying $15/month for dental and $7/month for vision, pre-tax, at a combined 30% marginal rate (federal + FICA + state):

- Combined monthly premium: $22.00
- Monthly tax savings: ≈ $6.60
- Annual tax savings: ≈ **$79.20**

It's a modest amount individually, but stacked with a medical premium, HSA, and 401(k) deduction, these pre-tax benefits can meaningfully shrink your taxable wages over a year.

## Standalone Plans vs. Bundled Plans

Some employers bundle dental and vision into the main medical plan; others offer them as standalone voluntary benefits you opt into separately during open enrollment. Either way, if deducted through payroll under the cafeteria plan, the tax treatment is identical.

## When Dental/Vision Premiums Are NOT Pre-Tax

- You purchase a standalone individual dental/vision plan outside of work — paid with after-tax dollars (though self-employed individuals may be able to deduct these as part of the self-employed health insurance deduction).
- Your employer doesn't offer a cafeteria plan, so the deduction defaults to post-tax.
- You're covering a domestic partner who isn't a tax dependent — that portion of the premium may be treated as imputed taxable income.

## Using an FSA or HSA for the Rest

Routine cleanings and eye exams are often covered by insurance, but **out-of-pocket costs** like orthodontia, premium lens coatings, or LASIK surgery are qualified expenses you can pay with FSA or HSA funds, adding another layer of tax savings beyond the premium itself.
