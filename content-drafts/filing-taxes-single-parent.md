## The Benefits Stack Up More Than You'd Expect

Filing as a single parent involves navigating several overlapping tax benefits at once — filing status, the Child Tax Credit, the Child and Dependent Care Credit, and the Earned Income Tax Credit all interact, and claiming them correctly can meaningfully change your refund or balance due.

## Step One: Confirm Your Filing Status

Most single parents qualify for **Head of Household**, not Single, as long as they pay more than half the cost of keeping up a home and a qualifying child lives with them more than half the year. HOH's $22,500 standard deduction (2025) versus Single's $15,000 is the single biggest lever available before credits even enter the picture.

## The Child Tax Credit

Worth up to **$2,000 per qualifying child** under age 17, with up to $1,700 refundable per child as the Additional Child Tax Credit even if you owe no tax. Phases out starting at $200,000 of AGI for Head of Household/Single filers — well above most single-parent incomes, so most qualify for the full amount.

## Child and Dependent Care Credit

If you pay for childcare so you can work or look for work, you can claim a credit on up to **$3,000 of expenses for one child** or **$6,000 for two or more**, at a rate between 20–35% depending on income. A single parent earning under $15,000 gets the full 35%; most working single parents fall closer to the 20% rate as income rises, still worth $600–$1,050 per child.

## Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)

A refundable credit that can be worth thousands of dollars for low-to-moderate income workers with children. For 2025, a single parent with one child can earn up to roughly $49,000 and still qualify for a partial credit; with two children, the ceiling is higher still. The EITC phases in as income rises from zero, peaks, then phases out — it's specifically designed to supplement earnings for working parents rather than non-working households.

## Worked Example

A single parent, Head of Household, with one 8-year-old child, earning $42,000 and paying $4,000 in after-school care:

| Benefit | Amount |
|---|---|
| Standard deduction | $22,500 |
| Child Tax Credit | $2,000 |
| Child Care Credit (20% of $3,000 cap) | $600 |
| EITC (approximate, 1 child) | ~$1,800 |

These credits alone can reduce federal tax liability to near zero and potentially produce a substantial refund, independent of any withholding.

## Claiming the Right Dependent After a Separation

Only one parent can claim a child as a dependent in a given year. The default IRS rule gives the claim to the parent the child lived with for the **greater number of nights**. Divorced or separated parents can agree to let the non-custodial parent claim the Child Tax Credit via **Form 8332**, but the custodial parent still generally keeps Head of Household status and the EITC regardless of that agreement.
