## Adoption Comes With Its Own Dedicated Tax Credit

Beyond the standard Child Tax Credit available to any parent, adoptive parents can claim the **Adoption Tax Credit** — one of the largest credits in the tax code, specifically designed to offset the often-substantial cost of adoption.

## How the Adoption Tax Credit Works

For 2025, you can claim a nonrefundable credit of up to **$17,280 per child** for qualified adoption expenses, including:

- Adoption agency and attorney fees
- Court costs
- Travel expenses (including meals and lodging) while pursuing the adoption
- Re-adoption expenses for finalizing a foreign adoption domestically

The credit phases out for modified adjusted gross income between **$259,190 and $299,190** (2025 figures) — most adopting families qualify for the full amount.

## Nonrefundable but Carries Forward

Because the credit is **nonrefundable**, it can only reduce your tax liability to zero — it won't generate a refund beyond what you owe. However, any unused portion **carries forward for up to 5 years**, so families who don't owe enough tax in the adoption year can still use the full credit over time.

## Special Needs Adoptions

If you adopt a child from U.S. foster care whom a state has determined has "special needs" (not necessarily a medical diagnosis — it can include being an older child or part of a sibling group), you can claim the **full credit amount regardless of your actual expenses**, even if you spent very little out of pocket.

## Employer Adoption Assistance

Many employers offer adoption assistance programs. Up to $17,280 (2025) of employer-provided adoption benefits can be excluded from your taxable income — separate from the credit, though you can't double-count the same dollar of expense for both benefits.

## Example

A couple finalizes an international adoption in 2025, spending $22,000 in qualified expenses. Their credit is capped at $17,280. If their tax liability for the year is only $9,000, they use $9,000 of the credit and carry forward the remaining $8,280 to apply against future years' tax bills.

## After the Adoption Finalizes

Once the adoption is legally finalized, the child becomes eligible for all the same benefits as a biological child:

| Benefit | Amount (2025) |
|---|---|
| Child Tax Credit | Up to $2,000 per child |
| Dependent exemption on W-4 (Step 3) | $2,000 per child, reduces withholding |
| Dependent Care FSA / Credit | Same rules as any dependent |

## What You Need to Claim It

File **Form 8839** (Qualified Adoption Expenses) with your return, and keep documentation — receipts, court orders, and agency statements — for at least three years in case of an IRS inquiry.
