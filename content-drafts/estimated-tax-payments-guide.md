## Paying Tax As You Earn, Without a Paycheck Doing It for You

Employees have taxes withheld automatically every pay period. Freelancers, independent contractors, landlords, and anyone with substantial investment or business income don't have that automatic withholding — so the IRS requires them to make **quarterly estimated tax payments** instead, covering both income tax and self-employment tax as the income is earned.

## Who Must Pay Estimated Taxes

You generally need to make estimated payments if you expect to owe **$1,000 or more** when you file, after subtracting withholding and refundable credits. This typically applies to:
- Self-employed workers and freelancers
- Landlords with significant rental income
- Investors with large capital gains or dividend income
- Retirees whose pension/IRA withholding doesn't cover their full tax liability

## The 2025 Due Dates

| Payment Period | Due Date |
|---|---|
| Jan 1 – Mar 31 | April 15, 2025 |
| Apr 1 – May 31 | June 16, 2025 |
| Jun 1 – Aug 31 | September 15, 2025 |
| Sep 1 – Dec 31 | January 15, 2026 |

Despite the "quarterly" label, these periods are uneven in length — the second period is only two months, reflecting how the IRS structured the calendar decades ago.

## Two Ways to Calculate What You Owe

**1. The safe harbor method (easiest).** Pay at least **100% of last year's total tax liability** (110% if your prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000), split evenly across four payments. This guarantees no underpayment penalty regardless of how this year's income actually turns out, even if you have a much bigger year.

**2. The current-year estimate method.** Project this year's actual income, deductions, and credits using Form 1040-ES, paying 90% of your actual current-year liability. This can mean smaller payments if your income is down from last year, but requires more accurate forecasting and carries penalty risk if you underestimate.

## A Worked Example

A freelancer who owed $12,000 in total tax last year, with prior-year AGI under $150,000, can satisfy the safe harbor by paying exactly $3,000 each quarter this year — regardless of whether this year's income is higher, lower, or identical — and owes no underpayment penalty even if they end up owing significantly more when they file.

## Don't Forget Self-Employment Tax

Estimated payments for the self-employed need to cover not just income tax but the **15.3% self-employment tax** (Social Security and Medicare combined) on net business profit. This is frequently the larger of the two components for moderate-income freelancers and is easy to underestimate if you only think in terms of "income tax."

## How to Pay

IRS Direct Pay, the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS), debit/credit card (with a processing fee), or mailed vouchers from Form 1040-ES all work — IRS Direct Pay is free and gives an immediate confirmation.
