## The Core Numbers

The IRS adjusts 401(k) limits annually for inflation. For **2025**, the employee elective deferral limit is **$23,500** — up from $23,000 in 2024. This is the maximum you personally can contribute from your paycheck, combining pre-tax and Roth 401(k) contributions.

## Catch-Up Contributions

If you're 50 or older by the end of the calendar year, you can contribute an extra **$7,500**, bringing your total to **$31,000** for 2025.

## The New "Super Catch-Up" for Ages 60-63

SECURE 2.0 introduced an enhanced catch-up for workers aged **60 through 63**: instead of the standard $7,500 catch-up, eligible participants can contribute an extra **$11,250**, for a total employee limit of **$34,750** in 2025. This window closes once you turn 64, reverting to the standard catch-up amount.

## The Combined Employer + Employee Limit

There's a second, higher limit that includes employer contributions (matching and profit-sharing) alongside your own: **$70,000** for 2025 (or $77,500 with the standard catch-up, $81,250 with the super catch-up for ages 60-63). Few employees hit this limit through matching alone, but highly compensated employees at generous-matching employers should be aware of it.

## Summary Table

| Category | 2025 Limit |
|---|---|
| Employee deferral (under 50) | $23,500 |
| Employee deferral (50+) | $31,000 |
| Employee deferral (ages 60-63) | $34,750 |
| Combined employee + employer (under 50) | $70,000 |
| Combined employee + employer (50+) | $77,500 |

## Looking Ahead to 2026

The IRS typically announces the following year's limits each October, based on inflation data (the Chained CPI). Limits have risen nearly every year this decade, generally in $500-$1,000 increments for the base deferral limit. Check irs.gov each fall for the official 2026 figures before adjusting your contribution elections for the new plan year.

## What Happens If You Over-Contribute?

If you accidentally exceed the limit (common when switching employers mid-year, since each employer's payroll system tracks its own limit independently), you must request a corrective distribution of the excess before the tax filing deadline, or face double taxation on that amount.
