## Most Tax Moves Have a December 31 Deadline

Unlike filing itself, which you can do as late as mid-October with an extension, almost every tax-saving **action** must happen by December 31 of the tax year. Waiting until April just means filing accurately for decisions you already locked in — it's too late to change most of them by then.

## Retirement Contributions: Know Which Deadline Applies

| Account | Contribution deadline |
|---|---|
| 401(k) employee deferral | December 31 (through payroll only) |
| Traditional/Roth IRA | Tax filing deadline (mid-April next year) |
| SEP IRA (self-employed) | Tax filing deadline, including extensions |
| HSA | Tax filing deadline (mid-April next year) |

Notice that 401(k) contributions are the odd one out — once your last paycheck of the year processes, you cannot retroactively add more. IRA and HSA contributions, by contrast, can still be made in the new year and counted toward the prior tax year.

## Checklist: By December 31

- [ ] **Confirm 401(k) contributions are on track to hit your target** — check your last few pay stubs; payroll systems can't make retroactive contributions after year-end
- [ ] **Harvest investment losses** to offset gains, mindful of the 30-day wash-sale window
- [ ] **Make charitable donations**, including any bunching strategy for this tax year
- [ ] **Use remaining FSA funds** — most health FSAs have a "use it or lose it" rule, though some plans offer a grace period or limited carryover (check your specific plan)
- [ ] **Take Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** if you're subject to them — the penalty for missing an RMD deadline is steep (25% of the amount not withdrawn, reduced to 10% if corrected quickly)
- [ ] **Review your W-4** if your situation changed (marriage, new dependent, second job) so January's paycheck starts the new year correctly
- [ ] **Estimate whether you owe underpayment penalties** and make a Q4 estimated payment by January 15 if self-employed or under-withheld

## Checklist: Items With Later Deadlines (Don't Confuse These)

- [ ] **IRA contributions** — can wait until the filing deadline
- [ ] **HSA contributions** — can wait until the filing deadline
- [ ] **SEP IRA contributions** (self-employed) — can wait until the extended filing deadline

## A Worked Example

A married couple realizes in November they're $4,000 short of their planned $23,500-per-spouse 401(k) target. With two pay periods left in December, they increase their contribution percentage temporarily to capture as much of the remaining room as payroll allows before year-end — since any shortfall can't be made up through payroll in January for the prior year.

## Don't Forget State-Specific Deadlines

Some state tax credits (529 plan deductions, certain state-specific retirement credits) also require contributions by December 31, separate from the federal IRA/HSA extended deadline — check your specific state's rules if you're relying on a state deduction.
