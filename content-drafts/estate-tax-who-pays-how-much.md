## Fewer Than 1 in 500 Estates Ever Pay This Tax

The federal estate tax has one of the highest exemption thresholds in the tax code, which is why it generates outsized media attention relative to how few families it actually affects.

## The 2025 Exemption

For deaths in 2025, the federal estate tax exemption is **$13.99 million per person**. Thanks to "portability," a surviving spouse can use any unused exemption from the first spouse to die, effectively giving a married couple a combined exemption of **$27.98 million** if elected properly on a timely-filed estate tax return.

## What Counts Toward the Taxable Estate

Your taxable estate includes essentially everything you own at death: real estate, investment accounts, retirement accounts, life insurance proceeds (if you owned the policy), business interests, and personal property — minus debts, funeral costs, and certain deductions like charitable bequests and the marital deduction for assets left to a surviving spouse.

## Who Actually Pays It

The **estate itself** pays any estate tax owed, before assets are distributed to heirs — using **Form 706**. Beneficiaries don't personally write a check to the IRS for estate tax; the amount is simply deducted from what's available to distribute.

## The Tax Rate

| Taxable Estate Above Exemption | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| First $1 million over exemption | Graduated rates starting at 18% |
| Remainder | Up to 40% maximum rate |

## State Estate and Inheritance Taxes

About a dozen states impose their own estate tax, often with **much lower exemptions** than the federal threshold — some as low as $1–2 million. A separate handful of states impose an inheritance tax instead (paid by the beneficiary, not the estate). It's entirely possible to owe state estate tax while owing zero federal estate tax, so check your specific state.

## The Gift Tax Connection

The estate tax and gift tax share a **single, unified lifetime exemption**. Large gifts made during your life (above the $19,000 annual exclusion) reduce the exemption available at death. This is why wealthy individuals often use lifetime gifting strategies — to move appreciation out of their estate while the exemption is still generous.

## Example

A single individual dies in 2025 with a $16 million taxable estate. After subtracting the $13.99 million exemption, $2.01 million is subject to estate tax, taxed at graduated rates up to 40% — resulting in roughly $700,000–$800,000 in federal estate tax owed by the estate before the remaining assets pass to heirs.

## An Important Caveat on the Exemption

The federal exemption amount is set by legislation and has changed significantly over the past two decades — it was roughly $1 million in the early 2000s and has grown substantially since. Always verify the current year's exemption rather than assuming it stays fixed, since future legislation could adjust it.
