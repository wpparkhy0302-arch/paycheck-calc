## Two Forms That Used to Be One

Before 2020, non-employee compensation was reported on Form 1099-MISC. The IRS split that function off into a dedicated form — **1099-NEC** — and 1099-MISC now covers a narrower, different set of payments. Confusing the two is common, but they report fundamentally different things.

## Form 1099-NEC: Non-Employee Compensation

A business issues you a **1099-NEC** if it paid you **$600 or more** during the year for services as an independent contractor, freelancer, or consultant — essentially, any time you did work and weren't treated as an employee.

Common 1099-NEC situations:
- Freelance writing, design, or development work
- Consulting fees
- Independent sales commissions
- Gig work paid directly by a business (not through a third-party payment app)

## Form 1099-MISC: Everything Else

**1099-MISC** now reports categories like:

| Box | Payment Type |
|---|---|
| Box 1 | Rents (e.g., renting out business property) |
| Box 3 | Prizes and awards, certain other income |
| Box 6 | Medical and health care payments |
| Box 10 | Gross proceeds paid to an attorney |

If you're a freelancer being paid for your work, you should almost always expect a 1099-NEC, not a 1099-MISC — receiving a 1099-MISC for ordinary freelance services is often a payer error worth flagging.

## Both Report to the Same Place on Your Return

Regardless of which form you receive, if the payment was for self-employment work, it belongs on **Schedule C** as business income, and it's subject to self-employment tax. The form type doesn't change your tax treatment — the *nature* of the income does.

## What If You Don't Receive Any 1099 at All?

You still owe tax on the income. If a client paid you $450 for a project, they aren't required to issue a 1099-NEC (the threshold is $600), but you're still legally required to report that $450 as business income on Schedule C.

## The $5,000 Threshold Change for Payment Apps (Form 1099-K)

Separately, if you're paid through platforms like PayPal, Venmo, or Stripe, you may instead receive a **Form 1099-K** once payments cross the IRS threshold. This is a different form from both 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC, but income reported on it that relates to your business still lands on Schedule C.

## A Worked Comparison

| Scenario | Form Received | Where It Goes |
|---|---|---|
| $5,000 consulting fee from a client, paid by check | 1099-NEC | Schedule C |
| $2,000 in rent for a storage unit you lease out | 1099-MISC (Box 1) | Schedule E (rental income) |
| $3,500 in freelance design fees via PayPal Business | 1099-K | Schedule C |
