## One New Dependent, Several New Benefits

A new baby is the single life event that unlocks the most tax benefits at once. Here's exactly what changes and what paperwork it requires.

## Get a Social Security Number First

You can't claim a child as a dependent without a valid **Social Security Number (SSN)**. Most parents apply for one at the hospital as part of the birth registration process; if you didn't, apply through the Social Security Administration before filing your return — missing the SSN is the most common reason a dependent claim gets rejected or delayed.

## The Child Tax Credit

For 2025, the Child Tax Credit is worth up to **$2,000 per qualifying child under age 17**, with up to $1,700 potentially refundable (paid even if you owe no tax) as the Additional Child Tax Credit. The credit phases out starting at $200,000 of income for single filers and $400,000 for married filing jointly — most working families receive the full amount.

## Updating Your W-4

Immediately update your **Form W-4** with your employer. Step 3 of the W-4 lets you enter the number of qualifying children multiplied by $2,000 — this directly reduces the amount withheld from each paycheck, so your take-home pay increases right away instead of waiting for a refund next April.

## Dependent Care Benefits

If both parents work and pay for daycare, two options help:

| Benefit | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **Dependent Care FSA** | Set aside up to $5,000/year pre-tax through your employer for daycare costs |
| **Child and Dependent Care Credit** | A tax credit worth 20%–35% of up to $3,000 (one child) or $6,000 (two+) in care expenses |

You generally cannot double-dip — expenses run through an FSA can't also be claimed for the credit.

## Filing Status Changes

If you're unmarried and the child lives with you more than half the year, you may qualify for **Head of Household** status, which has a larger standard deduction ($22,500 in 2025) and more favorable brackets than filing Single.

## Example

A married couple with a baby born in November 2025 still gets the **full** $2,000 Child Tax Credit for the year — there's no proration based on the child's birth month. If their combined income is $90,000, updating their W-4 to reflect Step 3 could increase their combined take-home pay by roughly $150–$165 per month, rather than receiving it as a lump sum refund the following spring.

## Other Benefits to Check

- **Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)** — a new qualifying child can significantly increase EITC eligibility for lower and moderate incomes
- **529 college savings plan** — contributions aren't federally deductible but grow tax-free for future education costs
- **Employer dependent care benefits** — some employers offer direct subsidies in addition to FSA access
