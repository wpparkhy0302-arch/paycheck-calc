## The Deduction Only Helps If You Itemize

Charitable donations are only deductible if you itemize on Schedule A instead of taking the standard deduction. With the 2025 standard deduction at $15,000 (single) or $30,000 (married filing jointly), a modest amount of annual giving often isn't enough on its own to clear that bar — which is why strategy matters more than the raw act of donating.

## Cash Donations: The Basics

Cash gifts to qualified 501(c)(3) organizations are deductible up to **60% of your adjusted gross income (AGI)** in a given year. Amounts above that limit carry forward for up to five years. Keep a bank record or written acknowledgment for any gift of $250 or more — the IRS requires it to claim the deduction.

## Donating Appreciated Stock Beats Donating Cash

If you've held an investment for more than a year and it's gained value, donating the shares directly to charity (rather than selling and donating cash) lets you deduct the full fair market value **and** avoid paying capital gains tax on the appreciation entirely. The deduction limit for appreciated securities is 30% of AGI.

**Example:** You bought stock for $2,000 that's now worth $8,000. Selling it first triggers tax on the $6,000 gain. Donating the shares directly gives you an $8,000 deduction with zero capital gains tax owed.

## Bunching Donations to Clear the Standard Deduction

Rather than giving $6,000/year every year (never beating the $15,000 standard deduction as a single filer), bunching three years of giving — $18,000 — into one tax year lets you itemize and exceed the standard deduction that year, then take the standard deduction in the two lighter years.

## Donor-Advised Funds Make Bunching Practical

A donor-advised fund (DAF) lets you make one large deductible contribution now, get the tax deduction immediately, and then recommend grants to specific charities over the following years — solving the problem of wanting to bunch donations without rushing to pick every charity at once.

## Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs) for Those 70½+

If you're 70½ or older with a traditional IRA, you can transfer up to $108,000 (2025, indexed annually) directly from the IRA to a qualified charity. This counts toward your Required Minimum Distribution but is **excluded from taxable income entirely** — often more valuable than a standard itemized deduction.

## Non-Cash Donations (Goodwill, Clothing, Household Items)

Items donated to thrift-style charities are deductible at fair market value, not original purchase price. For any non-cash donation over $500, Form 8283 is required; over $5,000 generally requires a qualified appraisal.

| Donation type | Deduction basis | AGI limit |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | Amount given | 60% |
| Appreciated long-term stock | Fair market value | 30% |
| Household goods | Fair market value (used condition) | 50% (as ordinary income property) |
