## A Status Many Unmarried Parents Miss

Head of Household (HOH) is one of the most under-claimed filing statuses on the books. Unmarried taxpayers supporting a dependent frequently default to Single without realizing HOH offers a significantly larger standard deduction and wider, more favorable tax brackets for the same income.

## The Three Requirements

1. **You must be unmarried** (or "considered unmarried") as of December 31 — this includes being legally separated, divorced, or living apart from your spouse for the last six months of the year with a qualifying child.
2. **You must pay more than half the cost of keeping up a home** — rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, repairs, property taxes, and insurance count; a dependent's personal spending money does not.
3. **A qualifying person must live with you for more than half the year** — typically a child, but can be a qualifying relative in limited circumstances (a parent you support doesn't need to live with you if you pay more than half their household costs).

## 2025 Numbers: HOH vs. Single

| | Single | Head of Household |
|---|---|---|
| Standard deduction | $15,000 | $22,500 |
| 12% bracket ends at | $48,475 | $63,100 |
| 22% bracket ends at | $103,350 | $100,500 |

The wider lower brackets mean more of a Head of Household filer's income is taxed at 10% and 12% before reaching higher rates, compared to a Single filer with the same gross income.

## "Considered Unmarried" for HOH Purposes

This is the detail people miss most often: you don't need to be divorced to claim HOH. If you are still legally married but lived apart from your spouse for the final six months of the year, filed a separate return, and otherwise meet the qualifying-child and cost-of-keeping-up-a-home tests, the IRS treats you as unmarried for HOH purposes specifically.

## Worked Example

A single parent earning $55,000 with one child living with them full-time:
- As Single: taxable income = $55,000 − $15,000 = $40,000
- As Head of Household: taxable income = $55,000 − $22,500 = $32,500

That $7,500 difference in taxable income, at a 12% marginal rate, is roughly $900 in federal tax savings — before even factoring in the Child Tax Credit, which is available under either status but is easier to combine with HOH's lower bracket thresholds.

## Common Disqualifiers

- A dependent who doesn't meet the IRS's specific "qualifying child" or "qualifying relative" tests (age, residency, support, relationship)
- Splitting custody roughly 50/50 with an ex-spouse — only the parent the child lived with for the *greater* number of nights in the year can claim HOH
- Claiming a dependent who earned more than half their own support
