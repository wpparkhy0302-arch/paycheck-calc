## Your Filing Status Depends on One Date

Like marriage, divorce is judged on a single snapshot: your marital status on **December 31**. If your divorce is finalized by that date, you file as Single (or Head of Household, if eligible) for the entire year — even if you were married for the first 11 months.

## Alimony: The Rules Changed in 2019

This trips up a lot of people using outdated information. For any divorce or separation agreement executed **after December 31, 2018**, alimony (spousal support) is:

- **Not deductible** by the person paying it
- **Not taxable income** to the person receiving it

This reversed the pre-2019 rule, where alimony was deductible to the payer and taxable to the recipient. If your divorce agreement predates 2019 and hasn't been modified, the old rules may still apply — check your decree carefully.

## Child Support Is Never Taxable

Unlike alimony's history, child support has always been treated the same way: it is **not deductible** by the paying parent and **not taxable income** to the receiving parent, regardless of when the divorce occurred.

## Who Claims the Children?

Only one parent can claim a child as a dependent in a given tax year. By default, the **custodial parent** (the one the child lives with for more nights of the year) claims the child and the associated Child Tax Credit. Parents can agree to alternate years, but the non-custodial parent needs **Form 8332** signed by the custodial parent to claim the dependent.

| Benefit | Who Can Claim |
|---|---|
| Dependent exemption / Child Tax Credit | Custodial parent (or non-custodial with Form 8332) |
| Head of Household status | Custodial parent only, if paying >50% of home costs |
| Child and Dependent Care Credit | Custodial parent only, regardless of Form 8332 |

## Dividing Retirement Accounts

Splitting a 401(k) or pension in divorce requires a **Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO)**. Transfers made under a QDRO are not taxed at the time of transfer — tax is deferred until the receiving spouse withdraws the funds, just as it would be for the original owner.

## Property Division

Transfers of property between spouses incident to divorce are generally **tax-free** at the time of transfer. However, the receiving spouse inherits the original cost basis — so if you receive a house or stock as part of the settlement, you may owe capital gains tax later based on the *original* purchase price, not the value at the time of divorce.

## Example

A couple divorces in June 2025 with one child. The mother has primary custody. She files as **Head of Household** (lower rates and higher standard deduction than Single) and claims the Child Tax Credit, unless she signs Form 8332 giving that credit to the father.
